About

Bridging imaging science, computation and clinical translation.

I am a biomedical engineer and neuroimaging researcher working at the intersection of advanced MRI, machine learning and mental health. My research asks how we can make individual-level predictions that are scientifically robust, clinically interpretable and genuinely useful.

At the Melbourne Brain Centre Imaging Unit, I contribute to multidisciplinary research spanning psychiatric neuroimaging, healthy ageing, exercise neuroscience and imaging informatics. I also serve as a National Imaging Facility Informatics Fellow, supporting reproducible analysis and research capability across the imaging community.

Selected programmes

Lead programmePrecision psychiatryExplainable individual profiles

Affinity Scores

An explainable framework that represents each person by how strongly they resemble clinically meaningful reference populations—and reveals which features drive that resemblance. The programme extends from cross-sectional profiling to longitudinal trajectories and clinical decision support.

  • Explainable AI
  • Psychosis
  • Longitudinal modelling
  • Clinical translation
7T MRIExercise neuroscience

Train Smart

A multimodal imaging programme investigating how exercise influences brain health. My work covers reproducible processing and quality control across structural MRI, diffusion MRI, resting-state fMRI, arterial spin labelling and white-matter hyperintensities.

  • Healthy ageing
  • Exercise
  • Multimodal MRI
  • Imaging pipelines
Multi-siteQuantitative MRI

WIRED QSM

Collaborative quantitative susceptibility mapping research focused on robust processing and analysis of brain iron and tissue susceptibility across large imaging cohorts.
